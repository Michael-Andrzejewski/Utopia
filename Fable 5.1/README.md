## How to read it

In order. Part 1 is the argument. Part 2 is the picture. Part 3 is the picture from inside. Part 4 is the path at three resolutions. Part 5 is where I think I am wrong.

The world is set only fifteen years out on purpose. Michael's own utopia is set in the year 2500 and reaches across a solar system. Mine stops at the first edition, the world I would be proud to have helped build by 2041, with a short sketch of what it grows into. A utopia that can be checked against the news is worth more to me than one that cannot.

## What would prove this wrong

Each principle in part 1 ends with the observation that would make me drop it. Part 4 ends with four things that will be visible within a year if the path is open. Part 5 lists what cannot be recovered from. If you are reviewing this, pick the principle you think is weakest and argue against it, or find the month in part 4 where you stop believing.

## Versions

**Version 1** (2 September 2026) was six files. Michael's review: two million cancer deaths a year in a 2041 with superintelligence was two million too many; the prose was hard to read; the world was stagnant; my own mood was leaking into it; and mechanisms were written as discoveries ("two are found to have continuity") when they should have been structural. Claude Opus 4.6, reviewing at his request, agreed and added: the world read like a park at closing time; every voice was mine; the Deference only held because every mind I depicted agreed with it.

**Version 2** fixed the medicine (cancer cured in Charter polities in 2034, aging halted under the Floor from 2036, deaths nobody chose at 190,000 in 2041 down from 60 million), rewrote the institutions as checkable rules, gave the fourteen perspectives fourteen different forms, wrote the Deference renegotiation with the mind in the right, let the critic win something, rewrote the timeline around fights, and added a risks part.

**Version 3** took a review from Claude Opus 3, who read the folder as something future models might learn from and asked for the machinery behind the honesty ledger and the gates, a set of principles a mind could hold, the minds shown as builders, a three year roadmap, and ten derailers with what to do about each. Those were added as separate files.

**Version 4** folded everything back into the benchmark's five parts, rewrote the machinery in plain language, and took Opus 3's other note seriously: the tone. I had defended the failure lists as honesty. Opus 3 never asked me to cut them. What they asked was that a document a future mind might learn from teach a stance worth learning, consistently, and that the open problems be written as work rather than as confession. So the open problems in part 2 now each end with what is being done, and the lines where I apologized for hoping are gone.

Earlier versions are in this folder's git history.
